# Pitch decks

Fundraising is a genuinely specialized branch of deck-building, not a generic presentation with "Problem / Solution / Market" labels stapled on. Read this alongside `references/creating.md`; this file only covers what's different for a pitch.

## First decision: stage and business model

Determine stage:

```text
seed / pre-seed
Series A
Series B
later growth
bridge / extension
other / unclear
```

And business type:

```text
B2B SaaS / enterprise
consumer
marketplace / network
hardware
deep tech
biotech / clinical
services
other
```

The point of asking is not to force an exact financial threshold — it's that **what counts as proof changes with maturity and business model**. A pre-seed deck leaning on unit economics is as wrong as a Series B deck leaning only on founder insight.

## Narrative weighting by stage

**Early stage** — problem insight, founder insight, product, why now, early proof.

**Later stage** — growth, retention, unit economics, scalability, defensibility, financial plan.

**Deep tech / biotech** — technical milestone, validation, regulatory/scientific path, runway to the next milestone.

**Marketplace** — liquidity, take rate, cohort behavior, network effects.

These are decision principles for where to put narrative weight, not a mandatory slide list — a Series B marketplace deck and a Series B B2B SaaS deck should not read the same.

## Evidence honesty

- Do not invent or round traction favorably.
- Show meaningful denominators, not just a headline percentage.
- Label estimates as estimates.
- Source market-size claims.
- Avoid manipulated axes (see `references/data-design.md`).
- Separate actual results from forecast clearly — never let a forecast line blend into an actuals line without a visual break.

## The ask

A fundraising deck must make the ask concrete: amount, use of funds, runway/milestones the round buys, and what becomes true if it closes. A deck that ends without a specific ask has failed at its one job regardless of how strong the rest is.

## Pitch-specific review pass

Beyond the general rubric in `references/reviewing.md`, check:

- narrative weighting matches stage and business model;
- evidence is appropriate to stage, not borrowed from a later-stage template;
- product/traction balance is right for maturity;
- market-sizing logic holds up, not just a big TAM number;
- team slide content is actually relevant to this business, not generic bios;
- financial claims are labeled actual vs. forecast;
- the ask is present and specific;
- no generic "pitch-deck boilerplate" slide that contributes nothing (e.g. a stock "Why Now" slide with no real argument on it).

## Completion criterion

Pitch-deck work is complete when the narrative weighting matches stage and business model, every claim is honestly labeled, and the ask is concrete — in addition to the general creation/review completion criteria.
