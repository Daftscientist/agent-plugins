# Genres

Design conventions vary by deck genre. This helps select *behavior* — density, tone, evidence — without forcing a named visual style; combine with `references/design-atlas.md` for the visual language itself.

### Investor
**Audience expectation:** fast, high-signal, skeptical of unsupported claims.
**Density:** low per slide, high across the deck.
**Narrative:** see `references/pitch-decks.md` — stage/model-specific.
**Tone:** confident, precise.
**Evidence:** traction, market, team, honestly labeled.
**Trap:** generic boilerplate slides that satisfy a checklist but prove nothing.

### Board
**Audience expectation:** governance-level judgment, not a sales pitch.
**Density:** moderate; detail available but not forced onto the slide.
**Narrative:** status → risk → decision needed.
**Tone:** measured, direct about bad news.
**Evidence:** financials, risk register, clear asks.
**Trap:** burying the actual decision the board needs to make.

### Executive briefing
**Audience expectation:** time-constrained, decision-oriented.
**Density:** low; one point per slide, detail in appendix.
**Narrative:** situation → so-what → recommendation.
**Tone:** confident, concise.
**Evidence:** the minimum that supports the recommendation.
**Trap:** background context crowding out the actual recommendation.

### Consulting / strategy
**Audience expectation:** rigorous, structured argument.
**Density:** higher than most genres; detail is expected.
**Narrative:** hypothesis → analysis → implication → recommendation.
**Tone:** analytical.
**Evidence:** frameworks, data, comparative analysis.
**Trap:** frameworks applied for their own sake rather than because they fit this problem.

### Sales
**Audience expectation:** relevance to their specific problem.
**Density:** low; heavy customization per prospect over generic breadth.
**Narrative:** their problem → your solution → proof → next step.
**Tone:** consultative, not a feature dump.
**Evidence:** case studies, ROI, customer proof.
**Trap:** a generic deck with the prospect's logo pasted on slide one.

### Product launch
**Audience expectation:** clarity on what's new and why it matters to them.
**Density:** low, high visual polish expected.
**Narrative:** problem → the new thing → what changes for the user.
**Tone:** energetic but substantive.
**Evidence:** real product screenshots, concrete before/after.
**Trap:** hype language with no concrete demonstration of the feature.

### Technical architecture
**Audience expectation:** precise, verifiable technical claims.
**Density:** higher; technical audiences want the real detail.
**Narrative:** requirements → design → tradeoffs → outcome.
**Tone:** precise, comfortable with technical vocabulary.
**Evidence:** diagrams, benchmarks, real tradeoff discussion.
**Trap:** oversimplifying to the point that experts stop trusting the deck.

### Research / academic
**Audience expectation:** rigor and reproducibility.
**Density:** high; methodology and caveats matter.
**Narrative:** question → method → result → limitation → implication.
**Tone:** precise, appropriately hedged.
**Evidence:** data, citations, methodology.
**Trap:** overstating certainty beyond what the method supports.

### Conference keynote
**Audience expectation:** engagement and memorability, live-narrated.
**Density:** very low per slide; the speaker carries detail.
**Narrative:** a clear through-line, often story-driven.
**Tone:** confident, often more visual/bold than a business deck.
**Evidence:** illustrative more than exhaustive — the talk carries the proof.
**Trap:** dense text slides that duplicate what the speaker is already saying.

### Training
**Audience expectation:** to actually learn and retain the material.
**Density:** moderate, paced across many slides rather than crammed.
**Narrative:** concept → example → practice/check.
**Tone:** clear, patient, consistent structure per topic.
**Evidence:** worked examples.
**Trap:** covering too much per slide because it "fits," at the cost of retention.

### Annual review / report
**Audience expectation:** comprehensive but navigable.
**Density:** moderate to high, well-sectioned.
**Narrative:** highlights → detail by area → outlook.
**Tone:** credible, not oversold.
**Evidence:** real numbers, YoY comparisons.
**Trap:** every metric shown as "up and to the right" regardless of what actually happened.

### Portfolio
**Audience expectation:** judging quality and range of work.
**Density:** low text, high visual quality.
**Narrative:** work speaks first, context second.
**Tone:** confident, restrained.
**Evidence:** the work itself, well-presented.
**Trap:** captions/process explanation overshadowing the actual work.

### Nonprofit / public sector
**Audience expectation:** credibility, impact, responsible use of resources.
**Density:** moderate.
**Narrative:** need → intervention → impact → accountability.
**Tone:** sincere, evidence-based, not overproduced.
**Evidence:** outcomes data, real stories with consent, funding transparency.
**Trap:** emotional appeal with no concrete outcome data to back it.

### Company all-hands
**Audience expectation:** honesty and relevance to their day-to-day.
**Density:** low; this is spoken-first content.
**Narrative:** where we are → what changed → what it means for you.
**Tone:** direct, human.
**Evidence:** real numbers, real examples from the org.
**Trap:** corporate-speak that avoids saying anything specific.

### Proposal
**Audience expectation:** a clear, biddable plan.
**Density:** moderate to high; specifics matter for evaluation.
**Narrative:** understanding of the problem → proposed approach → scope/cost → why us.
**Tone:** confident, specific.
**Evidence:** relevant past work, concrete scope.
**Trap:** vague scope that could apply to any vendor's proposal.

### Case study
**Audience expectation:** a credible, specific story of results.
**Density:** low to moderate.
**Narrative:** context → challenge → approach → result.
**Tone:** concrete, avoids generic praise language.
**Evidence:** real, specific numbers and quotes.
**Trap:** a result stated without the context needed to judge whether it's actually impressive.
