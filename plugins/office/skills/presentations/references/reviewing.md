# Reviewing a deck

Purpose: critical visual and narrative review, whether requested standalone or as the SEE stage of `SKILL.md`. This is a flat expert rubric plus a short procedure — read `references/quality-gates.md` alongside this for the final pass-through checklist.

## Procedure

1. Inspect the presentation outline.
2. Render a whole-deck preview/contact sheet.
3. Review sequence and consistency across the whole deck first.
4. Identify suspect slides.
5. Inspect those specific slides individually.
6. Classify each issue found.
7. Repair (see `references/editing.md` for mutation discipline).
8. Run a fresh, whole-deck preview pass after repairs.

Do not fix slide 1 the moment its first flaw is spotted, before you've seen the rest of the deck — an early fix made in isolation can conflict with a pattern you'd have noticed on slide 6.

## Review dimensions

**Narrative.** Does slide order tell a coherent story? Does each slide advance it? Are there repetitions? Are the transitions between ideas comprehensible without a narrator filling gaps?

**Purpose.** Can the main point of each slide be stated in one sentence? Is the focal evidence on the slide actually supporting that point, or just decorating it?

**Hierarchy.** Is the first thing the eye lands on the most important thing? Can the slide be understood rapidly? Are there competing focal points fighting for attention?

**Legibility.** Text size, line length, contrast, labels, footnotes, dense tables — can all of it actually be read at presentation scale?

**Density.** Too much content, needless card grids, excessive labels, every pixel filled, insufficient negative space.

**Alignment.** Do edges actually align? Is the grid intentional? Do baselines and columns feel coherent, or are things "almost aligned" in a way that reads as sloppy rather than deliberate?

**Rhythm.** Repeated spacing values, repeated typography behavior, deliberate slide-to-slide variation vs. accidental random layout switching.

**Contrast.** Scale, weight, tone, position, whitespace, and shape — not only color — used to answer "what matters first?"

**Imagery.** Quality, crop, aspect ratio, relevance, consistency. No stretched logos or screenshots.

**Data.** Chart choice, axis honesty, units, labels, the important series actually highlighted, no decorative chart junk.

**Tables.** Readable, meaningful column hierarchy, comparisons highlighted, not a spreadsheet-screenshot aesthetic unless that's genuinely the intent.

**Consistency.** Palette, title positioning, margins, motif, footer/page numbering if used, icon/image treatment — all consistent unless variation is deliberate.

**Technical.** Overflow, clipping, off-slide objects, missing images, hidden accidental content, placeholder text, unresolved validation warnings.

## Completion criterion

Review is complete only when every slide has been visually accounted for. "Looks generally good" on a skim is not sufficient — every slide needs either a specific classified issue or an explicit pass.
